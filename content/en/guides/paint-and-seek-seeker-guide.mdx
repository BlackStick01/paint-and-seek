## Mastering the Hunt: Essential Paint and Seek Seeker Tips

If you're looking to outsmart the best hiders in Paint and Seek, then you've come to the right place. This guide is tailored for seekers who are ready to take their game to the next level. By focusing on the nuances of the game, you'll learn how to hunt shapes, not just colors, and emerge victorious. Let's dive into the essential tips and strategies that will help you become a formidable seeker.

## Hunt Shapes, Not Colours

One of the fundamental rules of seeking is to hunt for shapes, not just colors. A skilled hider can perfectly match the color of their surroundings, making it nearly impossible to spot them by color alone. Instead, look for inconsistencies in shape. For instance, a limb that crosses a surface edge, an outline that doesn't match any object in the room, or asymmetry in something that should be uniform are all giveaways of a painted player. Remember, real objects are consistent, so a missed detail on the second look is a sign of a hider.

## Don't Spam Your Shots

It's tempting to fire at anything that looks slightly off, but this strategy is detrimental to your health. Each missed shot costs you precious health, and continuously testing objects will weaken you before you find a real target. Instead, slow down and rely on your eyes. Notice the detail that doesn't fit, confirm it, and then take your shot. When you do fire, do so with certainty, as every shot matters and should be earned.

## Clear Zones, Don't Chase Guesses

The timer in Paint and Seek can make random searching feel productive, but it often leaves corners unchecked. To be effective, split the map into zones in your mind and clear them one at a time. Focus on one wall, one cluster of objects, one high angle, and one escape route before moving on. Make sure you know why each zone is clear, and don't leave anything to chance.

## Be Suspicious of the Too-Perfect

As players improve, they start hiding in plain sight where nobody bothers to look. The more ordinary a spot seems, the less it gets checked, making it the perfect hiding spot for a skilled hider. Train your eye to distrust anything that looks too clean or too perfect. Stage art is messy, so if a corner of the room looks edited, there's a good chance someone has been hiding there.

## Change Your Angle

A single angle can make a disguise look flawless, but two angles usually break it. After scanning a suspicious shape, sidestep or climb to shift the relationship between the body and the background. If the shape slides differently from the wall or prop behind it, you've likely found a player. This is particularly effective against hiders who paint for the entrance view, as they'll look perfect from the spawn side and completely wrong from a few steps around.

## Read the Lighting

Lighting mistakes are some of the easiest tells to spot once you're looking for them. A reflection where the surface should be matte, a shadow falling the wrong way, or a highlight that doesn't match anything nearby are all signs of a painted body. These tells are almost like spotting a glitch, except this one involves a person.

## Get Low and Slow Down

Many of the best hiding spots are below eye level, such as floor zones and the bottoms of shelves. Drop into a crouch and inspect these areas, as that's exactly why good hiders use them. Additionally, lower your mouse sensitivity below your usual shooter setting to reward a slow, steady camera that catches the details a fast flick skips over.

## Play the Clock, Then Come Back

If one suspected hide is consuming too much of your time, don't get stuck on it. Mark the spot in your mind, move on, and clear the rest of the map first. A stubborn 50-50 is a win for every other hider while you're stuck on it. Once you've cut off the escape routes, circle back to suspicious areas for a second look. A hider who survived your first pass often relaxes too early, and that's when you catch the movement.
